Our mission

Accelerate science. Automate it. Understand how software changes it.

Every project we take on serves one goal: better science, faster. We are ultra-focused on producing rigorous publications — about how technology reshapes research, and about the clinical breakthroughs that quality data makes possible.

What we are working toward

Three goals, one direction.

01

Accelerate research

We automate the routine of science — data collection, documentation, registries — so researchers and clinicians spend their time on discovery, not on paperwork.

02

Measure the impact of software

Our own tools are research subjects. We study how new software affects the quality, speed, and rigor of science — and publish what we learn.

03

Publish what matters

High-quality publications are the output we optimize for: on technology in science, on colorectal cancer, and on the questions that better data finally lets us answer.

Where it is happening

Each of our projects advances the same mission — from national cancer data to physician training.

iLARS · National Registry

From patient monitoring to a national colorectal cancer registry

iLARS began as smart monitoring for patients after oncological surgery. It has grown into something larger: the national colorectal cancer registry — structured, high-quality data collected at national scale. That data is the foundation for strong publications and, ultimately, breakthroughs in how colorectal cancer is understood and treated.

SimuFlow · Simulation Centre

Raising the quality of science and physician training in Lithuania

At the simulation centre, SimuFlow models and optimizes clinical workflows. The result is twofold: physicians train in better-designed scenarios, and the research produced around them becomes more rigorous — improving the quality of medical science in Lithuania.

Dr. Filler · Automation

Giving clinicians their time back

Dr. Filler automates clinical documentation, filling visit data directly into electronic medical records. Less administrative load means more attention for patients — and more capacity for research.
